# Founder Tip Tuesday — week of 2026-10-27 — voiceover

Authoritative spoken source is `script.json`.

## 1

It's Founder Tip Tuesday. Mutiny, a company that makes AI software for sales teams, sent an outbound offer to prospects this summer, and thirty companies said yes within an hour. Nobody at Mutiny had time set aside to deliver what it had promised. So Matt Ratchford, the growth marketing lead, paused the campaign and built every piece himself. He wrote it all up in a weekly series, and Emily Kramer reprinted it in her M-K-T-one newsletter.

## 2

The offer was a custom piece of sales material for each prospect, built with Mutiny's own product, so it worked as a demo too. Each one took real work, and Mutiny's whole sales team was one account executive, with nobody whose job was booking meetings. Sending that offer to everyone meant spending those hours on accounts that weren't a priority.

## 3

Ratchford's view is that at Mutiny's stage, what the company needs most is credibility with the next buyer. He gets it from customers worth naming: well-known logos on the website, and the stories of how they use the product.

## 4

That gave him a way to cut. Mutiny already had a list of four hundred top-priority accounts. He went through it himself and kept only the companies whose logos he would want on Mutiny's website, which left two hundred fourteen. Those get personal outreach, while everyone else is pointed to a free sign-up with credits to try the product alone.

## 5

He also set a bar for the shortlist: fifteen meetings and five sales opportunities from those two hundred fourteen accounts within six weeks.

## 6

Hard-tech companies make the same kind of offer with more at stake: a pilot, or a demo unit on loan, and each one costs far more than a piece of sales material. So use Ratchford's cut on your own pilot requests. List the customers you would most want to name on your website, and give the pilots and your own hours to them. Everyone else can get something that runs without you, like a recorded demo or a distributor.
